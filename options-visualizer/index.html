How call and put options work

An option is a contract on 100 shares (charts show per share). The buyer pays a premium for a right; the seller collects it and takes on the obligation. Premium = intrinsic value (what exercising would be worth now) + time value (the chance of becoming more valuable before expiry).

Call option

The right to buy shares at the strike price K. It gains value when the stock rises.

Payoff at expiry = max(S − K, 0)

  • Long call: loss limited to the premium; profit unlimited above breakeven K + premium.
  • Short call: profit limited to the premium; loss unlimited if the stock rallies. Breakeven is also K + premium.
  • Delta 0 to +1; rises toward 1 as the call goes deep in the money.

Put option

The right to sell shares at the strike price K. It gains value when the stock falls.

Payoff at expiry = max(K − S, 0)

  • Long put: loss limited to the premium; max profit (K − premium) × 100 if the stock goes to zero. Breakeven is K − premium.
  • Short put: profit limited to the premium; loss large but capped at stock reaching zero.
  • Delta −1 to 0; falls toward −1 as the put goes deep in the money.

The Greeks (shown per share for your position)

Delta
Change in option price for a $1 move in the stock. Also a rough probability of finishing in the money.
Gamma
How fast delta changes. Highest near the strike close to expiry. Long options have positive gamma.
Theta
Value lost per day from time passing. Negative for buyers, positive for sellers. Accelerates near expiry.
Vega
Change in price for a 1-point rise in volatility. Buyers benefit from higher volatility.
Rho
Change in price for a 1-point rise in interest rates. Calls gain, puts lose. Usually the smallest effect.

Educational tool, not investment advice. Black-Scholes assumes European exercise, constant volatility and no dividends.
